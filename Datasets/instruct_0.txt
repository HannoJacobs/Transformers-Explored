Question: What are the Ten Commandments and where can they be found in the Bible?
Answer: The Ten Commandments are the foundational moral laws given by God to Moses on Mount Sinai, found in Exodus 20:1-17 and Deuteronomy 5:6-21. They are: 1) No other gods before God, 2) No idols, 3) Don't take God's name in vain, 4) Keep the Sabbath holy, 5) Honor your parents, 6) Don't murder, 7) Don't commit adultery, 8) Don't steal, 9) Don't bear false witness, 10) Don't covet.

Question: Who was the first man created according to Genesis?
Answer: According to Genesis 1-2, the first man was Adam, created by God on the sixth day of creation. God formed him from the dust of the ground and breathed life into him. Adam was placed in the Garden of Eden and given dominion over creation.

Question: What is the story of Noah's Ark about?
Answer: The story of Noah's Ark (Genesis 6-9) describes God's judgment on a wicked world through a great flood. Noah, a righteous man, was instructed to build an ark to save his family and pairs of animals. After 40 days of rain, the flood covered the earth for 150 days. Noah and his family emerged to repopulate the earth, and God made a covenant with a rainbow as a sign.

Question: Who led the Israelites out of Egypt?
Answer: Moses led the Israelites out of Egypt after God called him at the burning bush. He confronted Pharaoh, brought the ten plagues, led the Exodus through the Red Sea, received the Ten Commandments at Mount Sinai, and guided the Israelites through the wilderness for 40 years, though he died before entering the Promised Land.

Question: What happened at the Tower of Babel?
Answer: The Tower of Babel incident (Genesis 11:1-9) occurred after the flood when people united to build a tower to reach heaven and make a name for themselves. God confused their language, causing them to speak different languages and scatter across the earth, explaining the origin of different nations and languages.

Question: Who was Abraham and what covenant did God make with him?
Answer: Abraham (originally Abram) was a patriarch called by God from Ur to become the father of many nations. God made a covenant with him (Genesis 12, 15, 17) promising to bless him, give him numerous descendants, and give his offspring the land of Canaan. This covenant included circumcision as a sign and formed the foundation for Israel's relationship with God.

Question: What is the significance of the burning bush in Moses' story?
Answer: The burning bush (Exodus 3) was God's miraculous appearance to Moses while he was tending sheep in Midian. The bush burned without being consumed, symbolizing God's presence. From it, God called Moses to lead Israel out of Egypt, revealed His name "I AM THAT I AM," and commissioned him despite his fears and objections.

Question: How did the Israelites cross the Red Sea?
Answer: After the tenth plague and Pharaoh's pursuit, God miraculously parted the Red Sea (Exodus 14) through Moses' outstretched hand, creating a dry path through the waters. The Israelites crossed safely, but when Pharaoh's army followed, God caused the waters to return, drowning the Egyptian army and saving Israel.

Question: What was the manna that fell from heaven?
Answer: Manna (Exodus 16) was miraculous bread from heaven that God provided daily for the Israelites during their 40 years in the wilderness. It appeared like frost, tasted like wafers with honey, and was gathered each morning (except Sabbath). It sustained the Israelites and taught them dependence on God.

Question: Who was Joshua and what was his role after Moses?
Answer: Joshua was Moses' faithful assistant who became Israel's leader after Moses' death. He led the conquest of Canaan, famously at Jericho where walls fell, divided the land among tribes, and served as a military and spiritual leader, encouraging the people to remain faithful to God's covenant.

Question: What happened at the Battle of Jericho?
Answer: The Battle of Jericho (Joshua 6) was Israel's first victory in Canaan. God instructed Joshua to march around the city walls for seven days with priests blowing trumpets. On the seventh day, after seven circuits and a loud shout, the walls miraculously collapsed, allowing Israel to capture the city and demonstrating God's power over fortified cities.

Question: Who were the judges of Israel and what was their purpose?
Answer: The judges (Judges 2-16) were leaders raised up by God during the period before Israel's monarchy. They included Deborah, Gideon, Samson, and Samuel. Their purpose was to deliver Israel from oppression by foreign nations, administer justice, and lead the people back to faithfulness to God during cycles of apostasy and deliverance.

Question: What is the story of Ruth and Naomi?
Answer: The Book of Ruth tells of Naomi, an Israelite widow who returned from Moab with her daughter-in-law Ruth, who chose to stay with her. Ruth worked in Boaz's fields and eventually married him through the ancient custom of redemption. The story emphasizes loyalty, providence, and is part of Jesus' genealogy, showing God's care for the marginalized.

Question: Who was Samuel and how did he become a prophet?
Answer: Samuel was Israel's last judge and first major prophet (1 Samuel). Born to Hannah through prayer and dedicated to God, he was raised by Eli the priest. God called him as a child in the temple, and he became a prophet who anointed Israel's first two kings (Saul and David) and served as a spiritual leader and judge.

Question: What was the relationship between David and Goliath?
Answer: David, a young shepherd boy, confronted the Philistine giant Goliath (1 Samuel 17) who challenged Israel's army. Armed only with a sling and faith in God, David defeated Goliath with a stone to the forehead, then beheaded him with his own sword. This victory demonstrated that God fights for the faithful regardless of odds.

Question: How did David become king of Israel?
Answer: David was anointed king by Samuel as a youth and served Saul as a musician and armor-bearer. After defeating Goliath, he became a military hero but fled Saul's jealousy. After Saul's death, David was anointed king of Judah, then united all Israel after seven years, establishing Jerusalem as capital and bringing the Ark of the Covenant there.

Question: What were David's psalms and what do they express?
Answer: David's psalms (many in Book of Psalms) are poetic prayers and songs expressing worship, repentance, thanksgiving, lament, and praise. They cover themes of God's character, human emotions, wisdom, and messianic prophecies. Many psalms are attributed to David and continue to be used in worship today.

Question: Who was Solomon and what was his famous request from God?
Answer: Solomon was David's son and Israel's third king, known for wisdom and wealth. When God offered him anything, he requested wisdom to govern well rather than riches or power (1 Kings 3). God granted this and more, making him the wisest man ever, as evidenced by his judgment between two women claiming the same baby.

Question: What happened to the kingdom of Israel after Solomon's death?
Answer: After Solomon's death (1 Kings 12), the united kingdom split due to his son Rehoboam's harsh policies. The northern ten tribes formed Israel under Jeroboam, while Judah and Benjamin remained under Rehoboam. This division led to idolatry, foreign invasions, and eventual exile for both kingdoms.

Question: Who were the major prophets and what were their messages?
Answer: Major prophets include Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel. Isaiah warned Judah of judgment and promised a coming Messiah. Jeremiah called for repentance and prophesied Babylon's invasion. Ezekiel ministered to exiles with visions of God's glory. Daniel interpreted dreams and visions about future kingdoms and God's sovereignty.

Question: What does the book of Job teach about suffering?
Answer: The Book of Job explores the mystery of suffering through Job's trials. Though Satan challenges that Job only serves God for blessings, Job maintains his integrity. The book teaches that suffering isn't always punishment for sin, that God is sovereign over suffering, and that wisdom comes through trusting God even in unexplained trials.

Question: What are the Proverbs and who wrote most of them?
Answer: Proverbs is a collection of wise sayings primarily written by Solomon (Proverbs 1:1), with contributions from others like Agur and King Lemuel. It teaches practical wisdom for living, covering topics like diligence, honesty, relationships, speech, and the fear of the Lord as the foundation of wisdom.

Question: What is the Song of Solomon about?
Answer: Song of Solomon (Song of Songs) is a poetic celebration of romantic love between a bridegroom (Solomon) and his bride. It describes courtship, marriage, and intimate love, using beautiful imagery of nature and physical affection. It's interpreted as celebrating human love as God's gift and as an allegory of Christ's love for the church.

Question: Who prophesied the coming of the Messiah?
Answer: Many Old Testament prophets foretold the Messiah, including Isaiah (virgin birth, suffering servant), Micah (Bethlehem birthplace), Zechariah (triumphal entry), Jeremiah (righteous branch), and Daniel (exact timing). The prophecies describe His birth, ministry, death, resurrection, and eternal reign.

Question: What are the prophecies about Jesus in Isaiah 53?
Answer: Isaiah 53 describes the "Suffering Servant" who would be despised, rejected, and pierced for our transgressions. It prophesies His silent suffering, substitutionary death for sins, burial with the rich, and resurrection. This chapter is fulfilled in Jesus' crucifixion and is quoted in the New Testament as referring to Christ.

Question: Who was John the Baptist and what was his role?
Answer: John the Baptist was Jesus' cousin, the son of Zechariah and Elizabeth, who prepared the way for Jesus' ministry. He preached repentance, baptized people in the Jordan River, and announced Jesus as "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." He was imprisoned and executed by Herod Antipas for confronting his sin.

Question: What is the story of Jesus' birth?
Answer: Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born to the virgin Mary in Bethlehem during Caesar Augustus' census. Angels announced His birth to shepherds, and wise men followed a star to worship Him. Joseph and Mary fled to Egypt to escape Herod's massacre of infants, then settled in Nazareth.

Question: Where was Jesus born and what were the circumstances?
Answer: Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in a humble stable because there was no room in the inn. His birth fulfilled Micah's prophecy. Mary, a virgin, gave birth after an angel announced the conception to her. Shepherds and wise men came to worship the newborn King.

Question: What was Jesus' first miracle?
Answer: Jesus' first miracle was turning water into wine at the wedding in Cana (John 2:1-11). At Mary's request, Jesus had servants fill six stone jars with water, then commanded it to be drawn out as wine. This demonstrated His divine power and was the beginning of His miraculous signs.

Question: What did Jesus teach about the Kingdom of God?
Answer: Jesus taught that the Kingdom of God is both present and future - already here in His ministry but not yet fully realized. He described it as a priceless treasure, taught the Lord's Prayer for its coming, and explained parables showing its growth and ultimate triumph over evil.

Question: What is the Sermon on the Mount?
Answer: The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7) is Jesus' most comprehensive teaching, delivered on a hillside to His disciples and crowds. It includes the Beatitudes, teachings on salt and light, the law's fulfillment, teachings on murder, adultery, divorce, oaths, revenge, love for enemies, giving, prayer, fasting, money, worry, and judging others.

Question: What are the Beatitudes?
Answer: The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3-12) are eight blessings Jesus pronounced at the beginning of the Sermon on the Mount: blessed are the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the meek, those who hunger for righteousness, the merciful, pure in heart, peacemakers, and those persecuted for righteousness. They describe the character of Kingdom citizens and promise future reward.

Question: What is the parable of the Good Samaritan?
Answer: The Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37) tells of a man beaten by robbers who was ignored by a priest and Levite but helped by a Samaritan. Jesus taught that our "neighbor" is anyone in need, regardless of social or ethnic differences, challenging the lawyer's question about who deserves love.

Question: What is the parable of the Prodigal Son?
Answer: The Parable of the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11-32) describes a younger son who demanded his inheritance, squandered it in wild living, then returned home repentant. His father welcomed him with celebration, showing God's forgiveness. The older brother represents self-righteous resentment, teaching God's unconditional love for the repentant.

Question: What is the parable of the Mustard Seed?
Answer: The Parable of the Mustard Seed (Matthew 13:31-32) compares the Kingdom of God to a tiny mustard seed that grows into a large tree providing shelter for birds. It illustrates how the Kingdom starts small but grows dramatically, becoming a blessing to many despite humble beginnings.

Question: What is the parable of the Sower?
Answer: The Parable of the Sower (Matthew 13:3-23) describes a farmer sowing seed that falls on different soils: hard path (devil snatches), rocky ground (no root, withers), thorny ground (worries choke), and good soil (produces abundantly). It teaches how people respond differently to God's word and the importance of receptive hearts.

Question: What did Jesus say about loving your neighbor?
Answer: Jesus summarized the law as loving God with all your heart, soul, and mind, and loving your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:37-39). He taught that our neighbor is anyone in need, as illustrated in the Good Samaritan parable, and that love fulfills the entire law.

Question: How did Jesus heal the blind man?
Answer: Jesus healed several blind men, including Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52) who called out to Him, and a man born blind (John 9) where Jesus made mud with spit and told him to wash in Siloam. These miracles demonstrated Jesus' power and fulfilled prophecies about the Messiah healing the blind.

Question: What happened at the Last Supper?
Answer: During the Last Supper (Matthew 26, Mark 14, Luke 22), Jesus celebrated Passover with His disciples, washed their feet as a servant, instituted communion ("This is my body... This is my blood"), predicted Judas' betrayal and Peter's denial, and taught about His impending death and the coming Holy Spirit.

Question: What is the significance of the crucifixion?
Answer: Jesus' crucifixion was the central act of redemption where He died as a substitute for sinners, bearing God's wrath for human sin. It satisfied divine justice, provided forgiveness, defeated death, and demonstrated God's love. The cross is the ultimate expression of God's grace and the foundation of Christian salvation.

Question: What happened at the resurrection of Jesus?
Answer: Jesus rose from the dead on the third day, appearing to Mary Magdalene, the disciples, and over 500 others. The empty tomb was discovered by women, angels announced His resurrection, and Jesus appeared in His glorified body, ate with disciples, and taught from Scripture before ascending to heaven.

Question: What did Jesus say to the disciples before ascending to heaven?
Answer: Before His ascension (Acts 1:6-11), Jesus told disciples to wait in Jerusalem for the Holy Spirit's power, commissioned them to be witnesses to Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and the ends of earth (Great Commission), promised His return, and blessed them as angels watched Him ascend into heaven.

Question: What happened at Pentecost?
Answer: At Pentecost (Acts 2), the Holy Spirit descended on the 120 disciples in Jerusalem with wind and tongues of fire. Peter preached to the crowd, 3,000 were baptized, and the church was born. The Spirit enabled speaking in tongues, fulfilling Jesus' promise and Joel's prophecy.

Question: Who was Peter and what was his role in the early church?
Answer: Peter (Simon) was a fisherman called by Jesus to be a disciple, becoming the leader of the Twelve. After denying Jesus, he was restored and became the chief spokesman at Pentecost. He led the early church, performed miracles, wrote epistles, and is considered the first pope by Catholics.

Question: What was Paul's conversion experience?
Answer: Saul (Paul) was a persecutor of Christians when Jesus appeared to him on the Damascus road (Acts 9) in a blinding light. Jesus identified Himself, Saul was struck blind for three days, then healed by Ananias. This transformed him from persecutor to apostle, preaching Christ crucified to Gentiles.

Question: What are the fruits of the Spirit?
Answer: The fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) are love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. These are qualities produced in believers' lives by the Holy Spirit's work, contrasting with the works of the flesh and evidencing spiritual transformation.

Question: What is the armor of God?
Answer: The armor of God (Ephesians 6:10-18) represents spiritual protection: belt of truth, breastplate of righteousness, shoes of gospel peace, shield of faith, helmet of salvation, sword of the Spirit (God's word), and prayer. It equips believers for spiritual warfare against evil forces.

Question: What does the book of Revelation describe?
Answer: Revelation describes apocalyptic visions John received about end times, including letters to seven churches, cosmic battles, the rise of Antichrist, Christ's return, final judgment, and the new heaven and earth. It uses symbolic imagery to encourage persecuted Christians and reveal God's ultimate victory.

Question: Who wrote the majority of the New Testament epistles?
Answer: Paul wrote the majority of New Testament epistles (13 of 21): Romans, 1-2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1-2 Thessalonians, 1-2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon. Other writers include Peter (2), John (3), James (1), Jude (1), and Hebrews (anonymous but possibly Paul).

Question: What is the Great Commission?
Answer: The Great Commission (Matthew 28:18-20) is Jesus' final command to disciples: "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you." It mandates worldwide evangelism and discipleship.

Question: What are the signs of the end times according to Jesus?
Answer: Jesus described end time signs (Matthew 24, Mark 13, Luke 21) including wars, famines, earthquakes, persecution, false prophets, increased wickedness, abomination of desolation, cosmic disturbances, and His visible return. These are birth pains preceding the end but not indicating exact timing.

Question: What is the meaning of the Lord's Prayer?
Answer: The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13) teaches disciples to address God as Father, pray for His kingdom and will, request daily provision, seek forgiveness while forgiving others, and ask protection from evil. It provides a model for balanced prayer focusing on God's glory and human needs.

Question: What did Jesus say about forgiveness?
Answer: Jesus taught radical forgiveness: forgive others as God forgives you (Matthew 6:14-15), forgive "seventy times seven" times (Matthew 18:21-22), and pray for those who persecute you (Matthew 5:44). Forgiveness reflects God's mercy and is essential for receiving forgiveness (Matthew 6:15).

Question: How did Jesus respond to the woman caught in adultery?
Answer: When confronted with a woman caught in adultery (John 8:1-11), Jesus challenged the accusers: "Let him who is without sin cast the first stone." When they left, He told her, "Neither do I condemn you. Go and sin no more." This showed mercy while calling for repentance.

Question: What is the story of Lazarus being raised from the dead?
Answer: Lazarus of Bethany was sick and died (John 11). Jesus arrived four days later, wept at the tomb, and commanded "Lazarus, come out!" Lazarus emerged alive, bound in grave clothes. This miracle demonstrated Jesus' power over death and led many to believe, but also prompted the plot to kill Jesus.

Question: What did Jesus teach about money and possessions?
Answer: Jesus warned that wealth can be a barrier to the Kingdom (Mark 10:25), taught that you cannot serve both God and money (Matthew 6:24), encouraged generous giving (Luke 6:38), and told the rich young ruler to sell all and follow Him. True riches are eternal, not material.

Question: What is the Golden Rule?
Answer: The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12) states: "Do to others what you would have them do to you." Jesus called this the summary of the Law and Prophets, emphasizing treating others with the same consideration and kindness you desire for yourself.

Question: Who were the twelve apostles?
Answer: The Twelve Apostles were: Peter (Simon), Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew (tax collector), James (son of Alphaeus), Thaddaeus (Jude), Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot (who betrayed Jesus). They were eyewitnesses of Jesus' ministry and foundational to the early church.

Question: What was Judas' role and what happened to him?
Answer: Judas Iscariot was the treasurer of the Twelve Apostles. He betrayed Jesus for 30 pieces of silver, identifying Him with a kiss in Gethsemane. Overcome with guilt after Jesus' condemnation, Judas returned the money and hanged himself (Matthew 27:3-10), fulfilling prophecy and showing the tragedy of unrepentant betrayal.

Question: What is the significance of the transfiguration?
Answer: The Transfiguration (Matthew 17, Mark 9, Luke 9) occurred on a mountain where Jesus' appearance changed, His clothes became dazzling white, and Moses and Elijah appeared. God's voice declared Jesus as His beloved Son. It foreshadowed Jesus' resurrection glory and confirmed Peter's confession of Jesus as Messiah.

Question: What did Jesus say about prayer?
Answer: Jesus taught persistent prayer (Luke 18:1-8), warned against hypocritical prayer (Matthew 6:5), emphasized praying in secret to the Father (Matthew 6:6), taught the Lord's Prayer as a model, and promised that whatever is asked in faith will be granted (Mark 11:24) when aligned with God's will.

Question: What is the meaning of the bread of life discourse?
Answer: In the Bread of Life discourse (John 6:25-59), Jesus declared "I am the bread of life" and taught that eternal life comes through eating His flesh and drinking His blood - meaning believing in Him and participating in communion. Many disciples left, but the Twelve remained, showing this teaching's difficulty.

Question: What are the 'I Am' statements of Jesus?
Answer: Jesus made seven "I Am" statements in John's Gospel: I am the bread of life (6:35), the light of the world (8:12), the door/gate (10:9), the good shepherd (10:11), the resurrection and the life (11:25), the way, truth, and life (14:6), and the true vine (15:5). Each reveals an aspect of His divine nature and mission.

Question: What is the new covenant in Jesus' blood?
Answer: Jesus instituted the new covenant at the Last Supper (Luke 22:20), saying "This cup is the new covenant in my blood." Unlike the old covenant based on animal sacrifices, the new covenant provides forgiveness through Jesus' sacrificial death, writes God's law on hearts, and grants the Holy Spirit to all believers.

Question: What happened at the Garden of Gethsemane?
Answer: In Gethsemane (Matthew 26, Mark 14, Luke 22), Jesus prayed intensely before His arrest, sweating blood in agony over the coming crucifixion. He asked God to "take this cup from me" but submitted to the Father's will. Judas betrayed Him with a kiss, and Jesus was arrested after Peter's sword attack.

Question: What was Pilate's role in Jesus' trial?
Answer: Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor who tried Jesus. Though finding no guilt, he succumbed to crowd pressure and sentenced Jesus to crucifixion to avoid insurrection (Matthew 27, Mark 15, Luke 23, John 19). He symbolically washed his hands, acknowledging Jesus' innocence but prioritizing political expediency.

Question: What is the significance of the empty tomb?
Answer: The empty tomb is the cornerstone of Christian faith, proving Jesus' resurrection bodily. It demonstrates His victory over death, validates His claims, and provides hope for believers' future resurrection. The tomb's emptiness, guarded by soldiers and sealed with a stone, eliminates natural explanations for the body's disappearance.

Question: What did the women find at the tomb on Easter morning?
Answer: Mary Magdalene and other women found the tomb empty with the stone rolled away (Matthew 28, Mark 16, Luke 24, John 20). Angels announced Jesus' resurrection and instructed them to tell the disciples. This discovery initiated the resurrection proclamation and led to Jesus' appearances to various witnesses.

Question: How many days was Jesus in the tomb?
Answer: Jesus was in the tomb for parts of three days: crucified and buried Friday afternoon, in the tomb all day Saturday, and resurrected early Sunday morning. This fulfilled the prophecy of being in the heart of the earth three days and three nights, though the exact timing varies by interpretation.

Question: What did Jesus say to Thomas about faith?
Answer: When Thomas doubted the resurrection (John 20:24-29), Jesus appeared and said, "Put your finger here; see my hands. Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe." Thomas exclaimed, "My Lord and my God!" Jesus replied, "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed."

Question: What is the Great Commission and who gave it?
Answer: Jesus gave the Great Commission before His ascension (Matthew 28:18-20): "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them... and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you." It mandates worldwide evangelism, baptism, and discipleship as the church's primary mission.

Question: What is the baptism of the Holy Spirit?
Answer: Baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit's empowering for ministry, promised by Jesus (Acts 1:5) and fulfilled at Pentecost. It equips believers with spiritual gifts and power for witnessing. Some traditions see it as part of conversion, others as a subsequent experience often accompanied by speaking in tongues.

Question: What languages were spoken at Pentecost?
Answer: At Pentecost (Acts 2:4-6), believers spoke in other tongues (languages) as the Spirit enabled them. Parthians, Medes, Elamites, Mesopotamians, Judeans, Cappadocians, Pontus, Asia, Phrygia, Pamphylia, Egypt, Libya, Cyrene, Rome, Cretans, and Arabs all heard the gospel in their native languages.

Question: What was Peter's sermon at Pentecost about?
Answer: Peter's Pentecost sermon (Acts 2:14-41) explained the tongues phenomenon as Joel's prophecy fulfilled, proclaimed Jesus as Messiah crucified and resurrected, appealed to Scripture (Psalms), convicted the crowd of crucifying their Messiah, called for repentance and baptism, and resulted in 3,000 conversions.

Question: How did the early church organize itself?
Answer: The early church (Acts 2:42-47, 4:32-37) devoted itself to apostles' teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, and prayer. They shared possessions, met daily in homes and temple, appointed deacons for practical needs, and leaders (elders/pastors) provided oversight with apostles having ultimate authority.

Question: What was the Council of Jerusalem about?
Answer: The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15) addressed whether Gentile converts needed circumcision and Old Testament law observance. Peter argued God's acceptance of Gentiles, Paul and Barnabas shared missionary experiences. The council decided salvation by grace through faith, requiring only essential moral laws for Gentile fellowship.

Question: What is Paul's teaching on grace vs. works?
Answer: Paul taught salvation by grace through faith alone, not works (Ephesians 2:8-9, Romans 3:28, Galatians 2:16). Works cannot earn salvation but are the fruit of faith. Legalism (relying on works) denies Christ's sufficiency. Good works follow salvation as evidence, not means, of justification.

Question: What are the spiritual gifts mentioned in Corinthians?
Answer: Paul listed spiritual gifts (1 Corinthians 12:4-11): wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment, tongues, interpretation. He emphasized love as superior (1 Corinthians 13) and gifts for church edification (1 Corinthians 14), distributed sovereignly by the Spirit for mutual benefit.

Question: What is the love chapter in the Bible?
Answer: 1 Corinthians 13 is called the "love chapter" where Paul defines love's nature: patient, kind, not jealous or boastful, not proud or rude, not self-seeking, not easily angered, keeps no record of wrongs, doesn't delight in evil but rejoices in truth. Love never fails and is greater than gifts, faith, or hope.

Question: What does Paul teach about the resurrection?
Answer: Paul taught bodily resurrection (1 Corinthians 15): Christ's resurrection guarantees believers' resurrection to immortal bodies. Without resurrection, faith is futile. The resurrection body will be imperishable, glorious, powerful, and spiritual, transformed from corruptible to incorruptible at Christ's return.

Question: What is the book of Hebrews about?
Answer: Hebrews demonstrates Jesus' superiority over Old Testament institutions: better than angels, Moses, priests, sacrifices, and covenants. It warns against apostasy, encourages perseverance through suffering, and presents Jesus as the perfect high priest offering eternal redemption through His blood.

Question: What is the faith chapter in Hebrews?
Answer: Hebrews 11 is the "faith chapter" listing Old Testament heroes who lived by faith: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Rahab, Gideon, etc. It defines faith as "confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see," showing faith's power through trials and ultimate reward.

Question: What does James teach about faith and works?
Answer: James taught that genuine faith produces works (James 2:14-26). Faith without works is dead - like saying you're clothed but having no clothes. Abraham's faith was shown by offering Isaac; Rahab's by hiding spies. True faith transforms life and results in righteous actions.

Question: What is Peter's teaching about suffering?
Answer: Peter taught suffering as refining faith (1 Peter 1:6-7), following Christ's example (1 Peter 2:21-23), and glorifying God (1 Peter 4:12-16). Suffering tests genuineness of faith, develops perseverance, and prepares for eternal glory. Believers should rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings.

Question: What does John teach about love?
Answer: John taught God's love initiates ours (1 John 4:19), that love fulfills commandments (1 John 3:23), and distinguishes true from false believers (1 John 3:10-18). Love is not mere words but sacrificial action. God demonstrated love by sending Jesus (1 John 4:9-10), calling believers to love one another.

Question: What is the new heaven and new earth?
Answer: The new heaven and new earth (Revelation 21:1, Isaiah 65:17, 2 Peter 3:13) replace the current creation after final judgment. The old earth marred by sin passes away; God creates a perfect eternal dwelling where righteousness dwells, with no more death, sorrow, or pain.

Question: What are the seven churches in Revelation?
Answer: Revelation 2-3 addresses seven literal churches in Asia Minor: Ephesus (lost first love), Smyrna (persecuted), Pergamum (tolerant of false teaching), Thyrsus (deeds but incomplete), Sardis (dead reputation), Philadelphia (faithful remnant), Laodicea (lukewarm). They represent church conditions throughout history.

Question: What is the mark of the beast?
Answer: The mark of the beast (Revelation 13:16-18) is a symbol of allegiance to Antichrist during end times. Required for buying/selling, it represents ultimate rejection of God. The number 666 identifies the beast as human, not divine. Christians must refuse it despite persecution.

Question: What is the battle of Armageddon?
Answer: Armageddon (Revelation 16:16) is the final battle where nations gather against Israel. Kings assemble at Megiddo Valley, but Christ returns to defeat them (Revelation 19:19-21). It represents ultimate conflict between God's kingdom and worldly powers, ending human rebellion.

Question: What is the new Jerusalem?
Answer: The new Jerusalem (Revelation 21:2, 10-27) is the eternal dwelling place of God with His people: a city of pure gold, precious stones, 12 gates (tribes), 12 foundations (apostles). No temple needed as God dwells there; no night or pain. It symbolizes perfect fellowship between God and redeemed humanity.

Question: Who were Adam and Eve and what was their sin?
Answer: Adam and Eve were the first humans, created by God in His image (Genesis 1-2). Eve was deceived by the serpent to eat forbidden fruit from the tree of knowledge; Adam knowingly joined her. Their sin brought death, separation from God, and the fall of creation, requiring redemption through Christ.

Question: What is the meaning of the tree of life?
Answer: The tree of life (Genesis 2:9) in Eden symbolized eternal life and communion with God. After the fall, Adam and Eve were barred from it to prevent immortal sinners (Genesis 3:22-24). It reappears in Revelation 2:7, 22:2, 14, 19 as available to overcomers, representing eternal life through Christ.

Question: What is the curse on the serpent?
Answer: God cursed the serpent (Satan) for deceiving Eve (Genesis 3:14-15): "cursed above all livestock... you will crawl on your belly... enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel." This prophesied Christ's victory over Satan.

Question: What is the mark of Cain?
Answer: After Cain killed Abel, God marked Cain to protect him from vengeance (Genesis 4:15). The mark prevented others from killing him, showing God's mercy even to murderers. The exact nature of the mark is unknown, but it served as a divine protection sign.

Question: What is the story of Abel and Cain?
Answer: Abel and Cain were Adam and Eve's sons (Genesis 4). Abel offered acceptable sacrifices from his flocks; Cain's unacceptable offering from ground's fruit led to God's rejection. Cain's jealousy resulted in murdering Abel. God cursed Cain but marked him for protection, showing consequences of sin yet God's mercy.

Question: How long did Noah live?
Answer: Noah lived 950 years (Genesis 9:29), the longest recorded human lifespan. He was 500 when he fathered sons, 600 when the flood came. Post-flood lifespans decreased, suggesting environmental or genetic changes. Noah represents faithfulness amid universal wickedness.

Question: What was the rainbow's significance after the flood?
Answer: After the flood, God placed the rainbow as a covenant sign (Genesis 9:12-17) never again to destroy earth by flood. It symbolizes God's faithfulness to His promises and mercy toward humanity. The rainbow reminds both God and humans of this everlasting covenant.

Question: What is the story of Sodom and Gomorrah?
Answer: Sodom and Gomorrah were wicked cities destroyed by God for grievous sins (Genesis 18-19). Abraham interceded for righteous people. Lot's family was rescued, but his wife became a salt pillar by looking back. Only Lot and daughters survived, showing judgment on wickedness but mercy toward the faithful.

Question: Who was Lot and what happened to his wife?
Answer: Lot was Abraham's nephew who chose fertile Jordan valley land (Genesis 13). He lived in Sodom, was captured in war and rescued by Abraham. Angels warned him of Sodom's destruction; his wife disobeyed by looking back and became a salt pillar. Lot fathered Moabites and Ammonites through incest with daughters.

Question: What is the binding of Isaac?
Answer: God tested Abraham's faith by commanding sacrifice of Isaac (Genesis 22). Abraham obeyed, binding Isaac on altar at Moriah. At the last moment, God provided ram as substitute. This foreshadowed Christ's sacrifice and demonstrated Abraham's complete faith and obedience to God.

Question: Who was Rebekah and how did she meet Isaac?
Answer: Rebekah was Abraham's great-niece, chosen by God for Isaac. Abraham's servant prayed at a well for a sign; Rebekah drew water for him and his camels. She became Isaac's wife, bore Esau and Jacob, and helped Jacob deceive Isaac for blessing, showing God's providence in guiding relationships.

Question: What is the story of Jacob and Esau?
Answer: Jacob and Esau were Rebekah's twin sons (Genesis 25, 27). Esau sold birthright for stew; Jacob stole blessing through deception. Jacob fled, wrestled God at Peniel, became Israel. Brothers reconciled years later. Their story shows God's choice of younger over older and transformation through trials.

Question: What happened to Joseph in Egypt?
Answer: Joseph was sold into slavery by brothers, falsely accused by Potiphar's wife, imprisoned (Genesis 37-41). He interpreted Pharaoh's dreams, became prime minister, stored grain during plenty. Brothers came for food; Joseph revealed identity, forgave them, brought family to Egypt. His story shows God's providence through trials.

Question: What are the twelve tribes of Israel?
Answer: Israel's twelve tribes descended from Jacob's sons: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Joseph (divided into Ephraim and Manasseh), Benjamin. Each received land inheritance in Canaan. Levi became priestly tribe; Ephraim and Manasseh counted separately from Joseph.

Question: What is the story of Moses in the bulrushes?
Answer: Pharaoh ordered Hebrew baby boys killed (Exodus 1-2). Moses' mother hid him in papyrus basket among Nile reeds. Pharaoh's daughter found and adopted him. Miriam arranged Hebrew nurse (mother). Moses grew up as Egyptian prince but killed Egyptian, fled to Midian, married Zipporah, and encountered burning bush.

Question: What were the plagues of Egypt?
Answer: God sent ten plagues to compel Pharaoh's release of Israelites (Exodus 7-12): water to blood, frogs, gnats, flies, livestock disease, boils, hail, locusts, darkness, firstborn death. Each demonstrated God's power over Egyptian gods and escalated in severity, showing Pharaoh's hardening heart.

Question: What is the Passover and why is it celebrated?
Answer: Passover commemorates Israel's deliverance from Egypt (Exodus 12). Israelites marked doorposts with lamb's blood; death angel "passed over" these homes. They ate roasted lamb, unleavened bread, bitter herbs. It celebrates God's protection, foreshadows Christ's sacrifice as Passover lamb, and is celebrated annually.

Question: What happened at Mount Sinai?
Answer: At Mount Sinai (Exodus 19-34), God gave Ten Commandments, covenant terms, and tabernacle instructions. Moses ascended mountain, received laws, descended to find golden calf idolatry. God renewed covenant, Moses' face shone after encountering God. Sinai established Israel's relationship with God.

Question: What is the Ark of the Covenant?
Answer: The Ark of the Covenant (Exodus 25, 37) was a gold-covered wooden chest containing Ten Commandments tablets, Aaron's rod, manna. It symbolized God's presence (mercy seat between cherubim). Carried by Levites, it led Israelites, resided in tabernacle's Holy of Holies, later temple.

Question: What happened to the Israelites in the wilderness?
Answer: Israelites wandered 40 years for unbelief (Numbers 14). They complained about food/water, made golden calf, rebelled against leaders. God provided manna, quail, water from rock. Only Caleb/Joshua entered Canaan due to faith. Wilderness tested obedience, developed dependence on God.

Question: What is the story of Balaam and the donkey?
Answer: Balaam was a prophet hired by Balak to curse Israel (Numbers 22-24). God spoke through his donkey three times when Balaam beat it. Balaam blessed Israel instead of cursing, prophesying future Messiah. The story shows God's control over events and Balaam's mixed motives despite divine messages.

Question: Who was Deborah and what did she do?
Answer: Deborah was Israel's prophetess and judge (Judges 4-5). She judged from palm tree, summoned Barak to fight Canaanites. Barak insisted she accompany him; she agreed but prophesied victory to woman (Jael). Deborah's Song celebrates victory. She was one of few female leaders, showing God's use of women.

Question: What is the story of Samson and Delilah?
Answer: Samson was Nazirite judge with superhuman strength from uncut hair (Judges 13-16). Delilah betrayed him three times for Philistine money, learning his secret. She cut his hair while sleeping; Philistines captured, blinded, enslaved him. Hair grew back; Samson pushed down temple pillars, killing himself and 3,000 Philistines.

Question: What is the story of Hannah and Samuel?
Answer: Hannah was barren, prayed fervently for son at Shiloh (1 Samuel 1). Eli blessed her; she conceived Samuel, dedicated him to God. Samuel served Eli, God called him prophet. Hannah's Song praised God's faithfulness. Samuel anointed Israel's first kings, showing prayer's power and dedication to God.

Question: What was the Ark of the Covenant and what happened to it?
Answer: The Ark contained Ten Commandments, Aaron's rod, manna jar (Hebrews 9:4). Captured by Philistines, returned after plagues. David brought it to Jerusalem. Placed in Solomon's temple, later disappeared during Babylonian conquest (Jeremiah 3:16 suggests future return). Its location remains unknown.

Question: What is the story of David and Bathsheba?
Answer: David saw Bathsheba bathing, committed adultery with her (2 Samuel 11). She became pregnant; David arranged Uriah's death in battle. Nathan confronted David with parable; David repented. Child died but Solomon was born. This shows sin's consequences yet God's forgiveness of repentance.

Question: What was Solomon's wisdom demonstrated by?
Answer: Solomon asked God for wisdom, not riches (1 Kings 3). He judged between two women claiming same baby, exposing true mother. He wrote 3,000 proverbs, 1,005 songs, spoke on botany/zoology. His wisdom attracted Queen of Sheba, who tested him with questions and gifts.

Question: What happened to Elijah on Mount Carmel?
Answer: Elijah challenged 450 Baal prophets (1 Kings 18). Both prepared altars; Elijah's God consumed sacrifice with fire from heaven. Elijah executed false prophets. He fled Jezebel's threats, God fed him miraculously, spoke gently at cave, commissioned Elisha. This showed God's superiority over Baal.

Question: What is the story of Elisha and the widow's oil?
Answer: A widow owed debts, creditors threatened sons as slaves (2 Kings 4). Elisha told her to gather vessels, pour oil from jar. Oil multiplied miraculously until vessels filled. She sold oil, paid debts, lived on remainder. This showed God's provision and Elisha's ministry to the marginalized.

Question: What did Jonah experience in the whale?
Answer: Jonah fled God's call to Nineveh (Jonah 1). Storm arose; sailors threw him overboard. God prepared great fish swallowing Jonah for three days. Jonah prayed repentance; fish vomited him out. He preached Nineveh's repentance. Story shows God's sovereignty and mercy toward both rebellious prophet and wicked city.

Question: What is the story of Daniel in the lion's den?
Answer: Daniel prayed despite Darius' decree banning prayer to anyone but king (Daniel 6). Accusers caught him; Darius reluctantly threw him to lions. God shut lions' mouths; Daniel emerged unharmed. Darius praised Daniel's God, promoted him. This demonstrated faithfulness to God despite persecution.

Question: What is the handwriting on the wall?
Answer: During Belshazzar's feast, mysterious hand wrote on palace wall (Daniel 5). Daniel interpreted: God numbered kingdom, finished it; weighed, found wanting; divided, given to Medes/Persians. That night Belshazzar died, Darius conquered Babylon. This showed God's judgment on pride and idolatry.

Question: What is the story of Esther and Haman?
Answer: Esther became Persian queen through Hegai's favor (Esther 2). Haman plotted Jews' destruction after Mordecai refused bow. Esther risked life revealing identity, Haman hanged on gallows meant for Mordecai. Jews defended themselves, established Purim. God worked providentially through Esther's courage.

Question: What is the story of Nehemiah rebuilding the walls?
Answer: Nehemiah, cupbearer to Persian king, learned Jerusalem walls broken (Nehemiah 1). King sent him to rebuild. Faced opposition from Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem. Workers built with weapons ready. Completed in 52 days despite threats. Nehemiah reformed worship, tithes, Sabbath. Shows leadership and faithfulness.

Question: What is the prophecy of Jeremiah?
Answer: Jeremiah prophesied Judah's Babylonian destruction for idolatry (Jeremiah 1-25). Called "weeping prophet" for laments. Predicted 70-year exile, new covenant (31:31-34). Survived assassination attempts, dictated scroll to Baruch. His prophecies emphasize repentance, God's justice, future restoration.

Question: What is the story of Ezekiel's vision?
Answer: Ezekiel saw God's throne-chariot vision by Chebar River (Ezekiel 1): four living creatures, wheels within wheels, sapphire throne. Called to stubborn Israelites. Used symbolic acts: ate scroll, lay on sides, shaved head. Prophesied against nations, temple destruction, future restoration.

Question: What are the prophecies of Daniel?
Answer: Daniel interpreted Nebuchadnezzar's dream (kingdom statue), Belshazzar's writing, prophesied four kingdoms (Babylonian, Medo-Persian, Greek, Roman). Predicted Messiah's timing (70 weeks), end-time resurrection, Antichrist. Visions include ram/goat, 70 weeks, glorious man, kings of north/south.

Question: What is the book of Ecclesiastes about?
Answer: Ecclesiastes ("Teacher") explores life's meaning under sun. Solomon examines pleasure, wisdom, work, wealth - all "vanity" (meaningless) without God. Emphasizes fearing God, keeping commands, enjoying simple pleasures. Life's ultimate purpose found in relationship with Creator, not worldly pursuits.

Question: What does the book of Lamentations express?
Answer: Jeremiah laments Jerusalem's destruction by Babylonians. Uses acrostic poems expressing grief over siege, famine, death. Acknowledges sin caused judgment yet affirms God's mercy and faithfulness. Expresses hope in God's compassion despite suffering, showing appropriate response to tragedy.

Question: What is the prophecy of Zechariah?
Answer: Zechariah encouraged post-exile rebuilding, prophesied Messiah (triumphal entry on donkey), betrayal for 30 pieces silver. Visions include horsemen, measuring line, lampstands, flying scroll, woman in basket. Emphasizes spiritual renewal, God's presence, future kingdom with believing Gentiles.

Question: What is the prophecy of Malachi?
Answer: Malachi ("my messenger") addressed post-exile spiritual apathy. Accused priests of dishonoring God, people of robbing tithes, breaking faith with spouses. Prophesied Elijah's return before Messiah, coming day of judgment. Emphasizes pure worship, covenant faithfulness, ends Old Testament with promise of Messiah.

Question: What is the genealogy of Jesus?
Answer: Matthew traces Jesus from Abraham through David to Joseph (legal line). Luke traces from Jesus through Joseph back to Adam (biological line through Mary). Both include surprising women (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, Bathsheba). Genealogies establish Jesus' Jewish credentials, Davidic kingship, fulfillment of prophecies.

Question: What is the virgin birth?
Answer: Isaiah 7:14 prophesied virgin conceiving son called Immanuel. Gabriel told Mary she'd conceive by Holy Spirit without sexual relations. Joseph nearly divorced her until angel explained. Virgin birth proves Jesus' divine nature, sinlessness, unique identity as God-man, distinguishing Christianity from other religions.

Question: What is the story of the wise men?
Answer: Gentile wise men (magi) followed star to Jerusalem seeking newborn king (Matthew 2). Herod consulted chief priests about Bethlehem prophecy. Star led them to Jesus; they worshipped, gave gold/frankincense/myrrh. Warned by dream not to return to Herod, they departed differently. Represents Gentiles recognizing Jesus as King.

Question: What is the story of John the Baptist's birth?
Answer: Gabriel announced Zechariah and Elizabeth (barren, elderly) would have son (Luke 1). Zechariah doubted, struck mute until birth. Named John despite family tradition. Birth fulfilled prophecy of Elijah-like messenger preparing Messiah's way. John grew strong in Spirit, lived in desert until public ministry.

Question: What did Jesus do at age 12 in the temple?
Answer: At Passover, 12-year-old Jesus stayed in temple while parents left for Nazareth (Luke 2). Teachers amazed by His understanding and answers. Mary found Him, asked why He worried them. Jesus replied He must be about Father's business. This shows early awareness of divine mission.

Question: What was Jesus' baptism?
Answer: Jesus baptized by John in Jordan River (Matthew 3, Mark 1, Luke 3). John hesitated, but Jesus insisted to "fulfill righteousness." Heavens opened, Spirit descended as dove, Father's voice declared "This is my beloved Son." This inaugurated Jesus' public ministry and demonstrated Trinity.

Question: What was Jesus' temptation in the wilderness?
Answer: After 40 days fasting, Satan tempted Jesus (Matthew 4, Luke 4): turn stones to bread (physical need), jump from temple (test God), worship Satan for kingdoms (worldly power). Jesus countered each with Scripture. This demonstrated victory over temptation, prepared for ministry.

Question: What is the calling of the first disciples?
Answer: Jesus called fishermen Simon (Peter), Andrew, James, John by Sea of Galilee (Matthew 4, Mark 1, Luke 5). They immediately left nets to follow Him. Later called Philip, Nathanael (Bartholomew), Matthew (tax collector), Thomas, James (Alphaeus' son), Simon (Zealot), Judas Iscariot. Disciples learned Kingdom through relationship.

Question: What is the story of the wedding at Cana?
Answer: Jesus' first miracle at Cana wedding (John 2). Mary told Jesus wine ran out. Jesus turned water into wine using six stone jars. Wine was superior to previous. This demonstrated Jesus' divine power, provided abundantly, showed concern for celebration. Mary believed in His power despite early ministry.

Question: What is the story of the feeding of the 5000?
Answer: Large crowd followed Jesus to remote place (Matthew 14, Mark 6, Luke 9, John 6). Disciples suggested sending people away, but Jesus multiplied five loaves, two fish to feed 5,000 men plus women/children. Twelve baskets of leftovers. This demonstrated Jesus' provision, tested disciples' faith.
